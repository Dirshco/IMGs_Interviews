“The applicants who match well are not always the ones with the highest scores. They’re the ones who made the program feel like they belonged there before they even arrived.”

📋 The 5 things PDs actually care about

#1 Step scores
Step scores get you the interview. They don’t get you the rank.
After interview, we ask: “Can I work with this person at 3am? Will my residents respect them?” Focus on teamwork, resilience, and attitude.
#2 Personal statement
90 seconds scanning for self-awareness, a strong WHY, and genuine voice.
Avoid ChatGPT fluff. Show that you understand internal medicine (or your specialty) as a human — not a robot.
#3 Letters of recommendation
Detailed letter from a clinician who worked with you > title.
Generic superlatives signal the writer doesn’t know you. Seek meaningful mentorship.
#4 Interview presence
It’s not about your answers — it’s about professionalism, eye contact, listening, and grace.
30-min audition for a 3-year relationship. Are you gracious to coordinators & residents? Do you build conversation?
#5 Program research
Did you care enough to learn about us before walking in?
Read curriculum, social media, unique features. Asking questions answered on page 1 signals lack of effort.
Pro tip from the PD: “Your 'why this program' must be specific — built from real homework. Show that you belong here before you even arrive.”

✉️ Letters & research years

Letters: A detailed letter from an attending who worked with you for 4 weeks beats a generic chair letter. PDs notice specific stories — not just “great team player”.

Research years: Non-clinical (basic science/wet lab) can still be valued if productive (posters, manuscripts, first-author indexed papers matter). Show intellectual curiosity & discipline.

🔍 Many PDs appreciate meaningful contributions regardless of setting — but communicate how research shaped your clinical thinking.

🎯 PD Mirror: “Would I rank you?”

Test yourself on real PD priorities. Select the best response according to the PD’s perspective above.

1️⃣ During interview, the PD asks: “Why our program?” What impresses them most?

A) “Your program is highly ranked and will boost my career.”
B) “I read your resident advocacy curriculum and saw your GME newsletter about the new community clinic — I’d love to contribute.”
C) “I need a visa and you sponsor H1B, so I can stay in the US.”

2️⃣ What matters MOST after the interview invite?

A) Your USMLE step 2 CK score above 260
B) Professional presence, listening skills, and how you treat every staff member
C) Number of publications in high-impact journals

3️⃣ Personal statement red flag according to PD:

A) Using generic AI-generated language without real personal reflection
B) Being too short, only 300 words
C) Mentioning difficult patient encounter

📚 “Do your homework” – research tracker

Before any interview, complete at least 5 items from this list. Impress PDs with genuine knowledge.

Read program’s curriculum & rotation structure
Review recent faculty publications / social media posts
Learn about the city & community partnerships
Identify 2-3 unique program features (fellowship match, scholarly tracks)
Prepare 2 specific questions based on reading (not generic)
Watch program’s virtual tour / resident testimonials

✍️ PS authenticity scan

Does your statement sound like a real person? Use the PD's 90-second scan:

  • ✅ Demonstrates self-awareness & growth
  • ✅ Explains why internal medicine (or your specialty) specifically
  • ✅ Avoids clichés and robotic language
Insider tip: "If your PS reads like ChatGPT wrote it, I notice. If it reads like a real person who has thought about why they want this — you stand out."

💌 After the interview

Send a personalized thank-you note (email works). Reiterate why you are a great fit and one specific detail from the conversation.

PDs notice gracious follow-ups and professionalism. Do it within 24 hours.
